A purchase recorded against an envelope holds its whole budget back until the app is told the money has left the account; ticking one records its posting day, and the projection stops holding it back twice. **Purchases NEST under the thing they belong to** (plan step X-f2-c1, ruling R-EW): a grocery purchase and the grocery envelope belong to one block, and the flat list this replaces named each purchase's parent in a trailing fragment per line instead. Plan step X-f2-c2 puts that parent's OWN close tick inside the same block, and bills and transfer shadows in groups beside it -- which is why the heading level and the block wrapper are here now rather than being introduced with them, and why the producer's type is an ``OutstandingGroup`` rather than an envelope: neither a bill nor a transfer shadow is one. **The heading names the pay period as well as the parent**, because it has to: the recurrence engine materialises one row per template per period, so one envelope in two periods is two parents with one name and both can hold outstanding purchases at a single assertion. This is the INNER partial, rendered in two places from one file: the post-true-up prompt (``accounts/_reconcile_modal.html``, an out-of-band swap into ``#modal-mount``) and the permanent section on the cash account detail page. One implementation, so the two cannot come to say different things. Expected context: account -- the Account whose balance was asserted. outstanding -- a ``reconcile_service.OutstandingSet``: ``groups`` (each with ``name``, ``period_start`` / ``period_end``, ``purchases``, ``total``), ``purchase_count``, ``purchase_total`` and ``is_empty``. Already scoped to debit purchases on still-projected parents for THIS account, dated on or before ``reconciled_through``. Every figure is the producer's; this template computes none. reconciled_through -- the civil day of the account's latest asserted balance.
